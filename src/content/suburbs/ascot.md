## What usually goes wrong in Ascot

Most of Ascot is a thin ribbon of land squeezed between Great Eastern Highway and the river, so the highway does a lot of the damage. Rear-end crashes at the lights near Grandstand Road and Resolution Drive are common in peak hour, and they're the kind of job our [accident towing](/services/accident-towing-perth) crews deal with every week. We'll usually bill the insurer directly.

Race days at Ascot Racecourse bring a different set of problems. Cars sit for hours in big grass and gravel car parks, and when everyone leaves at once, a flat battery or a seized handbrake leaves someone stranded. Down by the water, people towing boats and trailers sometimes come unstuck on the soft edges of the riverside reserves.

## Around Ascot: roads, river and neighbours

Great Eastern Highway forms the southern edge of the suburb and is busy enough to have [its own page](/roads/great-eastern-highway). Grandstand Road runs along the racecourse, while Stoneham Street and Resolution Drive thread through the newer Ascot Waters estate. Head across the timber Garratt Road Bridge and you're in [Bayswater](/areas/bayswater). Inland, Ascot meets [Belmont](/areas/belmont) and [Redcliffe](/areas/redcliffe), with the airport not far beyond.

The streets around Ascot Waters are newer and fairly tight, with lots of visitor parking on the kerb. If your car is in a secure apartment car park, tell us the height limit before we arrive.

## Broken down in Ascot? Do this first

1. On Great Eastern Highway, pull into a side street or service road if the car will still roll. Don't stop in a turning lane.
2. At the racecourse, look for the nearest gate number or sign and tell us.
3. By the river, keep the car off the grass if you can. A wet lawn makes the pickup harder.
4. Have your rego and insurer details handy if there's been a crash.
