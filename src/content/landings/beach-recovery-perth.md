## Stop spinning the wheels

The natural reaction is to keep trying. Do not. Spinning wheels dig holes, and within a few attempts the car can be resting on its chassis or diffs instead of its tyres. That turns a simple recovery into a much harder one.

Instead:

1. Stop the engine and get out.
2. Look at how deep the wheels are and whether the underside is touching sand.
3. Check the tide and how close the water is.
4. Call us.

## Tide and safety

On beaches, the tide changes everything. A car stuck near the water line can be reached by waves within hours. If the tide is coming in, move yourself and your passengers above the high tide line first. Take phones, water and anything important out of the car.

On hot days, stay in shade, drink water and keep an eye on everyone. A car can be replaced. People cannot.

## Tyre pressure

Lowering tyre pressure is one of the best ways to drive on sand, because it widens the footprint and stops the tyres digging in. If you have the gear and know-how, letting some air out may help once the car is recovered. Never drive on bitumen at low pressures. Reinflate first, or have it towed.

## How we recover it

Recovery depends on where the car is and how deep it is stuck. Typically, the recovery vehicle stays on firm ground and the car is winched or pulled out with rated gear. Once on firm ground, it can be driven out if it is undamaged, or loaded onto a tilt tray and taken home or to a mechanic. Salt water and sand can cause damage, so if the car has been in the water, get it checked before driving.
