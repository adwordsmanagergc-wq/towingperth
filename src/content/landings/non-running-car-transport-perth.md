## Cars that will not drive themselves

Not every car that needs moving has broken down on the road. Plenty have been sitting for months or years: a project car in the shed, a car that failed and was never fixed, a vehicle left behind in a deceased estate, or a wreck heading to a buyer. They all have one thing in common. They will not drive onto a truck by themselves.

Call or text 0419 857 070 and we'll arrange the winch and tray for your dead car. This is part of our wider [vehicle transport](/services/vehicle-transport-perth) work across Perth.

## How we load a car that does not run

We use tilt tray trucks. The tray tilts to the ground and the car is winched up onto it with a cable. Once on, it is strapped down at the wheels and rides fully on the deck. A car that won't run doesn't need to roll an inch on the road, so nothing is added to the odometer and the tyres and driveline are spared.

For a car that has been parked a long time, a few things affect loading:

- **Seized brakes:** wheels that will not turn need more careful winching so the car slides up evenly.
- **Flat tyres:** we can usually load on flats, but pump them up first if you can, as it makes loading smoother.
- **Locked steering:** without keys, the steering may be locked and the car harder to guide.
- **Missing parts:** no engine, no gearbox or no wheels is fine to mention. We just need to know.

## Common jobs

- **Project cars:** bought a restoration or race project and need it home, to the panel shop or to the engine builder.
- **Dead cars:** a daily driver that has died in the driveway and is going to a mechanic or wrecker.
- **Deceased estates:** moving a car on behalf of the family or executor to a buyer, auction yard or storage.
- **Private sales:** you have sold a non running car and the buyer needs it delivered.
- **House moves:** the car is not roadworthy but has to come with you.

## Access at the pickup

Older cars often live in tight spots: a back shed, a narrow side path or a garage behind a gate. A tilt tray needs room to line up and lower the tray. Tell us:

- Where the car is and how far it is from the street.
- Whether there are gates, trees, low eaves or soft ground in the way.
- Whether the car rolls at all, and if the keys are available.

If it is very tight, photos sent by text help us judge it.
