## Heavy vehicles need the right truck

Dual cab utes and 4WDs like the Hilux, Ranger, LandCruiser, Patrol, D-Max and BT-50 are heavy before you add anything. Once you fit a steel bull bar, winch, canopy, drawer system, long range tank, dual batteries and roof rack, the weight climbs a long way past the brochure figure.

That's why we ask what's fitted before we send a truck. Every vehicle goes on a tilt tray, riding fully on the tray with no wear on the tyres, gearbox or transfer case and no kilometres added. Towing a 4WD on its wheels risks damage to the driveline, especially on full time 4WD systems, so we keep all four wheels off the road.

## Canopies, bull bars and lift kits

Accessories change how a 4WD loads. A bull bar with a winch adds weight over the front axle. A long canopy or rear wheel carrier adds overhang at the back. A lift kit and bigger tyres raise the height on the tray. When you call, let us know:

- Make, model and whether it's dual cab, single cab or wagon
- Bull bar, winch, canopy, drawers, roof rack and rooftop tent
- Lift height and tyre size
- Approximate overall height
- Whether it's towing a trailer or camper

## Common reasons for a 4WD tow

Overheating towing a camper up Greenmount Hill, a failed clutch, a snapped CV after a weekend off road, injector or turbo faults on a diesel and crash damage are all regular jobs. Some vehicles make it back onto the bitumen after a trip to Lancelin or the Perth Hills and then stop on the drive home along [Wanneroo Road](/roads/wanneroo-road) or the Great Eastern Highway.

## Towing, not recovery

This page covers towing a 4WD that's sitting on a road, driveway or other hard surface. If your vehicle is bogged on the beach, stuck in mud or can't get off a hills track, that's a recovery job first. See our [4WD recovery](/services/4wd-recovery-perth) service for that, and we can tow the vehicle afterwards if it can't be driven.

## While you wait

Park as far off the road as you can, switch on your hazard lights and put out a warning triangle if you have one. If you're towing a trailer or camper, leave it hitched unless it's safer to separate them. Keep everyone out of the traffic lane.
